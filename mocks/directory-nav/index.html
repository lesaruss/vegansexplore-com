Three more things that feel off

  • Words that mean two things. The Directory's "Media" is books and podcasts; the top menu's Media is our shows. The Directory's "Community" is nonprofits and rescues; the top menu's Communities is city hubs. That is likely why Cruises read as "in communities."

3 · What the research says

Show every door, in the visitor's words

Visible beats hiddenWhen navigation hides behind a menu, people use it less and find things less often than when it is on screen.NN/g, Pernice and Budiu, "Hamburger Menus and Hidden Navigation Hurt UX Metrics," 2016
Lead with top tasksA few tasks drive most visits. Put those on the surface, in the visitor's words.Gerry McGovern, "Top Tasks," 2018; NN/g, "Top Tasks Management"
Labels must predict the contentPeople follow labels that tell them what's behind them; vague or repeated labels make them stop.Pirolli and Card, "Information Foraging," Psychological Review, 1999; NN/g, "Information Scent"
Local sites lead with category doorsYelp's home page, Google Maps' category chips and Airbnb's category icons all show a short row of visible categories before any list.
Shallow tree, strong filtersTwo levels (door, then sub-section). Location, 100% Vegan and sort are filters, not more levels.NN/g, "Filters vs. Facets," 2014
Test the tree before buildingGive people find-it tasks on the bare structure and count first clicks, old tree vs new.NN/g, "Tree Testing"

10 · How we'll know

Test with outsiders, on phones, before we switch

Before the test
  • 1 Read what people already type into our search, and match door names to their words.
  • 2 A quick card sort: people group the sub-sections the way they think of them.
The test
  • First-time visitors who aren't members, plus members; you sit it out.
  • On phones and desktops, on the real screens.
  • Tasks as real situations that don't give the label away: "someone to cook for your party," "you heard a sanctuary has a show," "help with your diet," "a Vegan cruise."
  • Half see the old Directory first, half the new; half see noun names, half verbs.
  • A five-second test: show the front page, ask "what is this site for?"
We switch ifAt least 80% right first clicks on the new layout, better than the old, and most people describe more than "finding restaurants" after five seconds.
After 30 daysPer Directory visit: listing opens, saves, votes, and taps for directions or website, for food and non-food doors separately, and food doesn't drop.
