Hot Topic
Are ultra-processed vegan foods actually good for you?
Trending in the vegan community this week
MayaPre-Vegan Path9:04 AM
okay can we be honest for a second? when I first went vegan the vegan nuggets and burgers are what got me through. I would not have survived without them. the transition matters more than perfection.
that said I do eat more whole foods now than I did at the start. it just took time.
TheoSeasoned Vegan Path9:11 AM
I've been vegan for 22 years. The ultra-processed question wasn't even on the table when I started because the options barely existed. But I also think the fact that these products exist is a massive win for the movement. More people in, fewer animals harmed. That trade-off matters.
@Maya exactly. we weren't asking people to be perfect in 1999. we still shouldn't be.
RiverContent Creator Path9:18 AM
not gonna lie the "processed vegan food is bad" content performs well because it gives people a reason to feel superior. doesn't mean it's right. my audience responds way better to "here's something easy to try" than "here's why you're doing it wrong"
also the whole foods crowd isn't always thinking about who has access to them. that produce section at whole foods isn't showing up in every neighborhood.
DaniNonprofit Path9:24 AM
@River thank you. the communities I work with in Miami don't have the same access to fresh produce. a vegan patty at a fast food spot is meaningful progress for food deserts. let's not gatekeep health.
the question we should be asking is: who benefits from more vegan options in low-income neighborhoods? everybody. that's the justice frame here.
NoriEntrepreneur Path9:31 AM
from a business angle: the ultra-processed market is actually funding the R&D that will eventually give us better products. every Impossible Burger sold funds the next iteration. the entrepreneurs building in this space deserve credit not lectures.
also let's not pretend whole food vegan cooking is accessible for someone working 60 hours a week. systems thinking means meeting people where they are.
Also in the conversation
PascalCultural Bridge9:38 AM
in classical French cooking everything is processed in some way. the word "processed" is doing too much work in this conversation. what we should be asking is: was it made with care? does it feed someone? I reversed my Type 2 diabetes on plants. some came from a can. not ashamed.
there is always enough at my table. always.
JuneReal-Talk Guide9:45 AM
I grew up in rural Tennessee. the frozen vegan dinners kept me sane when I couldn't find anything else. anyone who wants to argue about it can come cook for my holiday table and we'll talk.

Hot Topic
Going vegan when your family isn't. How do you handle the table?
One of the most searched questions in the vegan community
JuneReal-Talk Guide8:52 AM
okay so I'll start. Thanksgiving at my parents' house in Tennessee. I bring my own dish. every year someone says "just try a little." three years before the commentary stopped. it does get easier.
being consistent without being preachy is the most persuasive thing you can do. my mom asked me for a vegan recipe last year. first time ever.
MayaPre-Vegan Path9:06 AM
my grandmother's recipe box is still on my shelf. I've veganized about half of what's in it. that was the move that got my family curious. not lecturing them. just showing up with something delicious that came from our food tradition.
if you try to out-argue your family you'll lose every time. if you out-cook them you might actually win.
TheoSeasoned Vegan Path9:14 AM
22 years in. I've watched three family members go vegan after years of resistance. not because I convinced them. because I stayed steady and they eventually got curious on their own. you cannot force a value shift. you can only live it clearly enough that people start to question.
also: choose your battles. the holiday table is not the hill.
RiverContent Creator Path9:22 AM
my whole thing went viral while my parents were still eating meat at the same table I was filming at lol. but they saw how much I cared. that changed the dynamic more than any argument would have.
the content angle nobody uses: film the family moments. the awkward questions, the wins, the first time someone tries your dish. it's real and people going through the same thing find it immediately.
PascalCultural Bridge9:30 AM
in France, food is how you say I love you. when I changed how I ate, my mother thought I was rejecting her. so I spent a whole summer veganizing her recipes with her. now she makes the cassoulet with white beans. she does not call it vegan. she calls it "Pascal's version." I'll take it.

Hot Topic
"But where do you get your protein?" - the question that never stops.
Still the #1 objection in vegan conversations worldwide
TheoSeasoned Vegan Path8:58 AM
I've answered this question ten thousand times. I stopped being annoyed by it and started seeing it as an opening. someone asking about protein is someone who is actually thinking about it. that's a better starting point than indifference.
my short answer: lentils, beans, tofu, tempeh, edamame, seitan. then I ask what protein sources they think are irreplaceable and we go from there.
NoriEntrepreneur Path9:05 AM
from a business angle this is an opportunity. if you run a vegan food business and you're not leading with protein content you're leaving money on the table. the question everyone asks is the gap you fill.
the people in your community asking this question are your first customers. answer it well and they'll buy.
MayaPre-Vegan Path9:13 AM
when I was transitioning I asked this to myself constantly. what helped was tracking for one week. after that I had my answer and never worried again. one week of data is worth more than any argument.
the first thing I say now is "more than you'd think." then I tell them what I had yesterday and watch their face change.
RiverContent Creator Path9:21 AM
protein content videos are mid. what actually works is "what I ate today" with a protein total at the end. my highest performing video in that category hit 2M views and it was literally just me eating.
people don't want a lesson. they want to see someone living the life and looking fine doing it.
DaniNonprofit Path9:29 AM
the communities I work with have been eating legumes for generations. black-eyed peas, rice and beans, lentil stews. the protein was there all along. the conversation should be about reclaiming those food traditions, not defending veganism against a question rooted in marketing.

A real one. The community is always divided on this.
MayaPre-Vegan Path9:02 AM
my partner isn't vegan. we figured it out. separate cutting boards, no beef about what's on the other person's plate. but I do cook most of the meals and he eats vegan at home by default at this point. slow drip works.
the thing that would actually be a dealbreaker for me isn't diet - it's if they mock the values behind it. that's different.
JuneReal-Talk Guide9:09 AM

TheoSeasoned Vegan Path9:17 AM
