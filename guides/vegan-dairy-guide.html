Why go dairy-free

Ditching dairy is one of the hardest parts of going Vegan. Culture, habit and taste all play a part. Here is what the research actually says.

Cheese is hard to put down

In a 2015 University of Michigan survey of about 500 adults, highly processed foods high in fat and refined carbohydrates were the ones people most often linked to out-of-control eating. Pizza ranked near the top; cheese on its own landed in the middle. Some researchers suspect casein, the main protein in milk, plays a role, but that has not been proven in people.

Schulte 2015, PLoS ONE

Many adults don't digest milk well

About a third of people in the US have trouble digesting lactose, the natural sugar in milk. It is much more common among Black, Native American, Asian American and Latino people than among people of European descent.

National Institutes of Health

Milk isn't the bone protector it's sold as

A 2015 review in The BMJ found that most studies show no link between drinking milk or eating dairy and fewer broken bones (25 of 28 milk studies, 11 of 13 dairy studies). Calcium matters, but dairy is not the only way to get it.

Bolland 2015, The BMJ

Health and the planet

What the cancer research shows

In a 2020 study of nearly 53,000 North American women, those who drank even 1/4 to 1/3 cup of cow's milk a day had about a 30 percent higher rate of breast cancer than women who drank none; soy milk was not linked to higher risk. A 2015 analysis of 32 studies linked more dairy, including low-fat milk and cheese, to a modestly higher rate of prostate cancer. Both are links, not proof, and the World Cancer Research Fund rates the evidence as limited.

Fraser 2020Aune 2015WCRF

Plants cover the nutrients

An ounce of peanuts has about as much protein as an ounce of cheddar, roughly 7 grams. Match cheddar's calcium (about 200 mg an ounce) with chia seeds plus calcium-set tofu or fortified soy milk. Choose kale and bok choy over spinach, whose calcium the body absorbs poorly. Federal guidelines count fortified soy milk and soy yogurt in the dairy group.

Heaney 1988Dietary Guidelines

Better for the planet

Per liter, cow's milk has about three times the climate impact of any plant milk and uses roughly ten times the land.

Poore and Nemecek 2018, Science

How milk is made

  1. A cow only makes milk after she gives birth. She gives milk for about 305 days after each calf, is usually bred again about three months after calving, and gets about two months off before the next birth.
  2. Calves are usually taken from their mothers within hours. In USDA's 2014 national dairy study, most operations removed calves less than 12 hours after birth.
  3. Male calves leave the farm within days. As of 2014, 97.6 percent of operations sold bull calves before weaning, at a median of 4 days old, to be raised for beef or veal.
  4. Most cows leave the herd young. US dairy cows spend on average about 35 months in the milking herd, fewer than three lactations. A cow can live up to about 20 years.
  1. Nearly all heifer calves have their horn buds removed. As of 2014, on 94.3 percent of operations; only 28.2 percent of those used pain relief. The industry added a pain-management standard in 2020.
  2. Tail docking was common and is being phased out. In 2014, a third of cows had docked tails. The industry began phasing it out in 2017.
  3. Most cows live on large farms. In 2022, 65 percent of US milk cows were on operations with more than 1,000 cows, up from 17 percent in 1997.

Make it at home

Nut milk

  1. Soak 1 cup raw nuts in water for 6 to 8 hours or overnight.
  2. Drain and rinse.
  3. Blend with 4 cups fresh filtered water on high for 60 to 90 seconds.
  4. Strain through a nut milk bag or clean towel, squeezing out all the milk.
  5. Refrigerate in a sealed bottle and use within 3 to 4 days.

Tip: save the pulp for crackers, nut flour or smoothies.

Banana ice cream

  1. Put frozen ripe bananas, frozen fruit of your choice and a few pitted dates in a blender.
  2. Add plant milk halfway up the fruit, then water to the top.
  3. Add 1 tbsp vanilla extract.
  4. Blend about a minute, until smooth.
  5. Top with mulberries and enjoy.
