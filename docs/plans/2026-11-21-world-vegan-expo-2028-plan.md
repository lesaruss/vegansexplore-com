## 1. The vision and positioning

*(Public copy. A sponsor, a city or a brand reads this first.)*

The World Vegan Expo is the meeting place of the Vegan world. For five days in January 2028, one host city becomes the center of Vegan food, business, culture and causes. Outside, the Food Village fills with local vendors, chefs and food trucks. Inside, the Exhibitor Hall lets people taste what the biggest brands are making next, and the World Food Pavilion brings select vendors from around the globe to one place, many of them for the first time. The Education Village puts the who's who of the Vegan world on stage: activism, food futures, business, marketing, health and culture. At night, the city turns into the Expo's dining room, with a different chef dinner every night and parties that carry the spirit of Miami's Seed Food and Wine Festival, where our founder spent seven years as marketing chair. On Saturday night, the Vegans Explore Awards celebrate the people, businesses and causes the community has vouched for. And anyone at home can watch it all live and on demand with the digital pass, which every ticket includes.

It happens every two years, like the World Cup. The time in between is the qualifying season: community nights, local Spotlights and the awards nominations that build city by city toward one week where everyone shows up. Businesses do not just buy a booth. They claim their space, starting with a free listing on Vegans Explore.

**Why the World Cup of the Vegan community:**
- **One global moment, not one more local fest.** Most Vegan festivals are local and annual. The largest reported ones draw tens of thousands (Toronto's Veg Food Fest reports about 40,000 a year; Tel Aviv's festival reported 40,000 to 50,000). The World Vegan Expo combines the festival, the trade show, the conference and the awards in one place, built to be the largest Vegan event ever held (target, to be proven by counted attendance).
- **Every two years makes it scarce.** A biennial event is a date people plan a trip around. It gives exhibitors and the host city two years of build-up.
- **Built on a living platform.** The Expo is not a one-week website. It sits on Vegans Explore, where the businesses, people, causes and events already live year round.
- **Earned, not bought.** The awards come from member vouches (one per member) plus an expert panel. The World Food Pavilion is juried. The Food Village is curated from the Directory.

**Positioning line options** (founder decides; recommendation: the first):
1. "The World Cup of the Vegan community. Every two years."
2. "The whole Vegan world, in one place."
3. "Claim your space."  *(for the business-facing campaign)*

## 3. Choosing the host city: the bid

### How it works
1. **Call for Host Cities.** Sent privately to the convention and visitors bureaus (CVBs) and city partners in our chapter cities. Public copy says "a Vegans Explore chapter city will host," never a count.
2. **Expression of interest.** Each city sends a short letter: venue options and dates held, hotel blocks, incentives, local partners.
3. **Shortlist** of three cities.
4. **Site visits** to the shortlist (Sean plus the producer).
5. **Final bid** with signed letters of support from the CVB and the venue.
6. **Decision** and host city agreement.
7. **Announcement** with the full launch (Section 14).

### Criteria and scoring (100 points)

| Criterion | Points | What we look for |
|---|---|---|
| Venue fit and availability Jan 19 to 25, 2028 | 20 | 250,000 to 400,000 sq ft of halls plus an outdoor plaza for the Food Village, meeting rooms for the Education Village, a ballroom for the Awards (1,500 seats) |
| January weather | 10 | The Food Village is outdoors. A warm, dry January (Sun Belt) or a covered outdoor space |
| Local Vegan business strength | 15 | Share of the city's Vegan businesses with a relationship with Vegans Explore (the 80% goal), claimed listings, community night results |
| Incentives and subvention | 15 | Cash or in-kind support from the CVB, city or state: venue discount, bid fund, marketing, police and permits |
| Hotel and air access | 10 | Hotel block at agreed rates, direct international flights for the World Food Pavilion |
| Community and partners | 10 | Active local clubs, nonprofits, sanctuaries, colleges, chef scene |
| Media market and tourism draw | 10 | A city people want to travel to in January |
| Cost to produce | 10 | Union and labor rules, venue in-house service rates, food and beverage exclusivity terms (critical: we need outside Vegan food vendors allowed) |

**The deal breaker to check first:** many convention centers hold exclusive food and beverage rights. Our whole event depends on outside Vegan vendors cooking and selling. Every bid must show in writing that the venue's caterer will waive or share exclusivity for our vendors, on what terms (Assumption: a commission on vendor sales is common; we cap it in the contract).

### What the host city and CVB provide (the ask)
- A venue rental discount or a bid fund (research: CVBs offer cash incentives, bid support funds, marketing dollars and convention center discounts; state programs such as Louisiana's Events Incentive Program and Illinois's Tourism Incentive Grant Program fund event attraction, and some CVBs cap incentives near $50,000 while larger programs go much higher).
- A hotel block with rebates or room-night incentives.
- Marketing: the city's tourism channels, airport and transit placements.
- Permits, police, fire marshal and street closures handled with one city contact.
- A welcome from the mayor at Opening Night, and the city's name in the event title ("World Vegan Expo 2028, [City]").
- Introductions to local sponsors (the region's hospital system, grocery chain, airline hub, sports teams).

**What the city gets:** room nights, visitor spending, a global food moment in a slow-season month, and a year-round listing of its Vegan scene on Vegans Explore.

### Timeline
| Step | Date (target) |
|---|---|
| Venue research and quiet holds placed | December 2026 to February 2027 |
| Call for Host Cities sent | March 1, 2027 |
| Expressions of interest due | April 15, 2027 |
| Shortlist of three | May 15, 2027 |
| Site visits | June 2027 |

| **Decision** | **August 16, 2027** |

**Timing risk (Internal):** large convention centers book years ahead, and a 14-month lead for a January event is short. That is why quiet venue holds start now, before the bid opens, without naming the event publicly. Founder decides (Section 17, decision 1).

## 5. Day by day: the run of show

**Recommendation: Thursday is trade only. Monday is industry plus full-event pass holders.** Founder decides (decision 4). Default if no override: as below.

### Thursday, January 20: Trade and Media Day
- **8:00 to 9:00** Badge pickup for trade, press, exhibitors, speakers.
- **9:00 to 10:00** Industry Keynote (Main Stage, streamed).
- **10:00 to 5:00** Exhibitor Hall and World Food Pavilion open to trade only: buyers, distributors, restaurant owners, investors, press. Buyer meeting lounge with booked 20-minute meetings.
- **All day** Business and Food Futures tracks.
- **2:00** Press walk with the host city.
- **Food Village** opens 11:00 to 7:00 to trade and the public (free entry outside, pay for food). This lets the city feel the Expo from day one and gives vendors a soft opening.
- **Night: Opening Night.** A welcome reception with the mayor and the host city, Food Village vendors and chefs serving tastings, live music. Ticketed (included in VIP and full-event passes).
- **Chef Dinner 1:** "The Host City Table," the city's best Vegan chefs cooking together.

### Friday, January 21: Public Day 1
- **10:00 to 6:00** Every village open to the public.
- **Main Stage:** Opening Keynote, Food Futures panel, a health panel.
- **Food Village:** 10:00 to 8:00, live cooking stage, Burger Battle heats.
- **Creators Stage:** live podcast taping.
- **Night: Chef Dinner 2:** "World Table," chefs from the World Food Pavilion cook one course each.
- **Late: Friday Night Party** (River's night): DJ, the Food Village after dark.

### Saturday, January 22: Public Day 2 (the biggest day)
- **10:00 to 6:00** Every village open. Kids and Family programming all day.
- **Main Stage:** the marquee panel ("the who's who"), the activism keynote.
- **Wellness:** morning workout, the lifting meet.
- **Food Village:** Burger Battle final, Dessert Battle.
- **Night: The Vegans Explore Awards** (Section 9). Red carpet 6:30, show 7:30, after-party 10:00.
- **Chef Dinner 3** is the Awards dinner itself: a seated dinner for VIP and table buyers, cooked by a celebrated chef with a guest chef team.

### Sunday, January 23: Public Day 3 (family and community day)
- **10:00 to 5:00** Every village open. Student and community discount day.
- **Community Hub:** club meetups, chapter cards, the 2030 "Where should we go next" board.
- **Main Stage:** Culture track, the documentary premiere (Section 10) at 2:00.
- **Night: Chef Dinner 4:** "The Plant-Based Steakhouse" or a dinner hosted by a celebrated restaurant, in a partner restaurant in the city.
- **Late: Sunday Night Party** (also the Exhibitor and Vendor thank-you).

### Monday, January 24: Industry and Impact Day, then Closing
- **9:00 to 2:00** Exhibitor Hall open to trade and full-event pass holders. Follow-up buyer meetings.
- **Education Village:** Business and Causes deep-dives, investor showcase (10 founders, 5 minutes each).
- **10:00** Impact Morning: volunteers and attendees go out in the host city (sanctuary visits, a food donation run from Expo surplus, a community garden). Ties to Causes.
- **12:00 Closing Brunch** (Chef Dinner 5 becomes a brunch, the last meal together): the 2030 announcement teaser, thanks to the host city, the handover flag (the 2030 host city, if chosen).
- **2:00 to 8:00** Load-out.

**Why Monday this way:** a five-day event that ends on a Monday loses the public, so we give Monday to the people who stay: trade, full-event holders and the Impact Morning. It also cuts one public day's staffing cost.

**Calendar checks (Assumption, confirm before booking):**
- The Winter Fancy Food Show (trade, usually January in Las Vegas) may fall close to our Thursday. Check the 2028 date; if it is the same week, we move trade outreach early and pitch "go from one to the other."
- Late January is NFL conference championship weekend (likely Sunday, January 23, 2028). It affects hotel rates and TV attention in two cities. Check which cities host before the decision.
- January is Veganuary month. That is a gift: we pitch the Expo as the Veganuary finale.

### Juried and curated selection
- **Exhibitor Hall:** open to every brand whose products on the floor are fully Vegan, with a short review (ingredients, claims, a sample).

- **World Food Pavilion:** juried by a panel of chefs, travel writers and community leaders. Applications open with the full announcement; invitations go to standouts the Directory already knows. Target: 15 or more countries.
- **The fully Vegan rule:** every item sold or sampled on the grounds is Vegan. A Pre-Vegan business may take part only with its plant-based items, and its sign says "plant-based (Pre-Vegan) options," never "Vegan options."

**Honor**
24. The Lifetime Impact Award (panel only)

### How winners are chosen
1. **Nominations** open to everyone with a free Vegans Explore membership (target: October 1 to November 30, 2027). A nominee needs a Directory listing or People page (we create it if missing; nominees are told).
2. **Eligibility check** by the team: Vegan (or the Pre-Vegan category), active, in good standing.
3. **Finalists:** the panel picks five per category from the nominations, weighted by impact.
4. **Member vouches:** one vouch per member per category, with Undo, from December 1, 2027 to January 7, 2028. The word is Vouch, never "vote."
5. **Panel score:** a panel of 15 (chefs, journalists, nonprofit leaders, business leaders, past winners from 2030 on) scores each finalist.
6. **Final result:** 50% member vouches, 50% panel score (founder decides the split; recommendation: 50/50, default 50/50). The Lifetime Impact Award is panel only.
7. **Audit:** abuse checks on vouches (new accounts, bursts from one source) using the platform's abuse watch, with the rules written before vouching opens.

### Impact criteria (the panel's score card)
- **Reach:** how many people or animals the work touched.
- **Quality:** the food, the product, the work itself.
- **Community:** what they give back locally.
- **Innovation:** something new.
- **Integrity:** the way they do it (labor, honesty, sustainability).

### The night
- Red carpet with the step-and-repeat (Awards Partner) and the Creators Stage team filming arrivals.
- Two hours, 24 awards, fast: 90 seconds per category, with short pre-made videos of each finalist.
- Two live performances.
- Streamed live on the digital pass; clips posted that night.
- Winners get a physical award (the suitcase mark, Vegans Explore approved) and a "Winner 2028" badge on their listing for two years.

## 17. The founder's decisions

Each one: the recommendation, why, and the default if Sean does not override.

1. **Place quiet venue holds now, before the bid opens.**
   Recommendation: yes. Ask CVBs in the warm chapter cities for tentative holds for January 19 to 25, 2028, without public naming. Why: a January 2028 convention center may be gone by the time the bid closes. Default: we start venue research and holds in December 2026.

2. **How the 80% goal gates the announcement.**
   Recommendation: the 80% relationship goal applies to the host city as a bid requirement; the full announcement goes out October 1, 2027. Why: holding it for every chapter city could push the announcement too late to sell a January event. Default: host city gate, October 1 announcement.

3. **How the Expo is funded until revenue arrives.**
   Recommendation: a founding presenting partner (with a 2030 renewal option) plus a host city advance, with a line of credit as backup; about $500,000 of bridge by September 2027. Why: venue and production deposits land before ticket money. Default: we pitch the founding presenting partner first.

4. **Which days are trade only.**
   Recommendation: Thursday trade and media only (the Food Village outside is open to all); Monday for trade and full-event pass holders plus the Impact Morning and the Closing Brunch. Why: buyers and press need a quiet floor, and a Monday public day would be thin. Default: as written in Section 5.

5. **Ticket pricing and fees.**
   Recommendation: Day $39, Weekend $89, Full Event $229, VIP $899, single dinner $225, Digital $39; early bird about 25% off until November 30, 2027; all-in prices with our own 5% fee absorbed. Why: priced above local fests and well under trade shows, with the free Food Village keeping the door open. Default: these prices.

6. **Whether Pre-Vegan businesses can take part.**
   Recommendation: yes, only with their plant-based items, in a marked Pre-Vegan Pavilion, signed "plant-based (Pre-Vegan) options," plus one Awards category for them. Why: it brings big names and their audiences without blurring what is Vegan. Default: yes, as described.

7. **Sponsors owned by companies that are not Vegan.**
   Recommendation: accept a Vegan brand owned by a larger company, as long as only the Vegan brand appears; decline sponsors whose own brand sells animal products. Why: much of the money in this market sits in those parent companies, and the community will judge the logos it sees. Default: Vegan brand only, parent name never shown.

8. **The Awards split.**
   Recommendation: 50% member vouches, 50% panel; Lifetime Impact panel only. Why: the community decides half, experts keep it fair. Default: 50/50.

9. **The host city decision date.**
   Recommendation: August 16, 2027, with the announcement October 1, 2027. Why: it leaves about five months to sell and produce, which is tight but workable with venue holds already in place. Default: these dates.

10. **The go or scale rule.**
    Recommendation: on September 30, 2027, if committed sponsorship is under $600,000 and booth deposits are under 100 booths, we scale to three days and one hall and keep the name and the Awards. Why: it protects the brand and the money; a smaller first edition beats a cancelled one. Default: this rule.
