# Group Partner program: a BOSS plan for Vegans Explore

Prepared by a community-program consultant (a composite expert lens, not a real person), 2026-11-19.
Inputs: the consultant brief, the full current Group Partner Guide text, the organizer-facing facts, the panel of five organizers, and web research (cited below; some pages could not be opened from this session, so a few points rest on search summaries and are marked).

---

## Step 0: Define the challenge

### The symptom
Five organizers read the guide. Four would take a meeting, one would pass, none would apply. Every one of them named the same gaps: no dollar amount, no guarantee the night happens, no say over sponsors, a share ladder that pays nothing, a form that feels like an audition.

### The root cause
The program asks the organizer to carry the risk, while the platform carries the upside.

Look at it from the organizer's side. Vegans Explore has about 69 members and Instagram posts that reach 60 to 130 people. A mid-size club has 400 to 2,500. So the organizer is the draw, and they know it ("you need my audience more than I need yours"). Yet the current guide asks them to:
- apply and be judged,
- announce a night that might be cancelled if sponsors do not come through, with their name taking the hit,
- accept sponsors they did not choose in front of members who trust them,
- consider moving off Meetup to earn "featured" status,
- read about a commission ladder that is paused,
- all for a contribution with no stated amount.

Every one of those puts the cost on the organizer and keeps the commitment soft on our side. Organizers in the Vegan community have watched platforms and brands come and go. Soft commitments read as "they want our list."

The fix is not better copy. It is moving the risk. **We commit first, in writing, with numbers. The organizer commits to one night they were already going to run.**

### Three deeper causes under that one
1. **The offer was built around what the platform needs** (members, ticketing, moving events here, the share ladder) **instead of the organizer's jobs**: fill the room, cover the venue, give regulars a great night, protect members' trust, and for activists, fund the work.
2. **Funding risk sits in the wrong place.** "Nights only go ahead if sponsors cover them" is a fair rule for the founder, but the guide lets that risk land on the club after it has announced. The fix is timing: no club announces until the money is already in.
3. **Nothing has happened yet.** No night, no recap, no organizer vouching. Trust in this community moves organizer to organizer. Until three nights happen and three organizers say "it was real," no page will create a line.

### Assumptions we are making (test them)
- A fixed amount around $150 a night is enough to matter to a small club and not so large it looks like buying them. (Test in the first five calls.)
- Fully Vegan brands will pay cash, not only give product, for a night with 40 to 100 Vegan guests. (Biggest risk. Test now, before organizers are pitched.)
- Organizers care more about control and certainty than about the size of the money. (The panel says yes.)
- The founder's personal relationships can open the first doors; the program has to open the rest.
- Most groups will keep Meetup, Partiful, Instagram or Facebook. That is fine; we do not need to replace them to be useful.

## B: Best practice

What proven programs did to recruit and keep organizers and groups. For each: the source, why it works, an example, and a citation. "General knowledge" marks anything I did not confirm in a source during this research.

### 1. Local ownership inside a standard format (parkrun)
- **What:** parkrun is a free, weekly, volunteer-led 5km. Every event uses the same simple structure; local teams own the running of it. It grew from 13 runners in one park in 2004 to thousands of events in more than 20 countries. It is funded mainly by sponsors and partners, so local organizers mostly only raise money to launch.
- **Why it works:** The organizer never pays, never carries the money risk, and gets a proven playbook. The brand stays in the background; the local event is the hero.
- **Lesson for us:** One standard night format, the club owns the night, the funding is handled centrally. Sponsors stay off center stage.
- **Sources:** Heart Foundation case study (https://www.heartfoundation.org.au/blueprint/case-studies/parkrun); Euronews, 2024 (https://www.euronews.com/2024/11/13/from-a-small-meetup-to-global-phenomenon-how-parkrun-got-10-million-people-obsessed-with-r); RSA lessons blog (https://www.thersa.org/blog/2019/12/social-movement-common-good-parkrun, seen in search results only; the page could not be opened from this session).

### 2. A network sponsor that pays the organizer's costs (Meetup Pro networks)
- **What:** Organizations such as the Python Software Foundation run Meetup Pro networks. Qualifying local groups join, and the network pays their Meetup fees. Network admins can show sponsors across groups; local organizers can still add their own sponsors to their own group. Meetup's separate Sponsorship Program pairs a sponsor with an organizer's in-person event under written terms (for example, the organizer cannot move the date or place without consent).
- **Why it works:** The group keeps its name and its own sponsors. The network removes a cost. The terms are written down both ways.
- **Lesson for us:** A Group Partner should feel like joining a network that pays for things, not a platform that wants to own them. Write the sponsor terms on both sides.
- **Sources:** Python Software Foundation, Meetup Pro (https://www.python.org/psf/meetup-pro); Meetup Help, Displaying a sponsor (https://help.meetup.com/hc/en-us/articles/4404960838797-Meetup-Pro-Displaying-a-sponsor); Meetup Sponsorship Program Organizer Terms (https://help.meetup.com/hc/en-us/articles/360030671992-Sponsorship-Program-Organizer-Terms-of-Service, seen in search results only; the page could not be opened from this session).

### 3. Brand-backed clubs where the club stays free and local (Lululemon ambassadors and run clubs)
- **What:** Lululemon's ambassadors are local instructors, studio owners and run club leaders. The program starts from an in-person relationship at a local store, not an online application. Ambassadors get product and support for their own events. Coverage of run clubs notes some are "powered by" a brand while staying free for members, and that some independent clubs turn sponsorship down entirely to protect their identity.
- **Why it works:** The brand earns its place by serving the leader's existing community. Selection is a relationship, not an audition.
- **Lesson for us:** Replace "Apply" with a conversation and a date. Expect some groups to say "no sponsors"; give them a version that has none.
- **Sources:** Lululemon, Community engagement (https://corporate.lululemon.com/our-business/innovation/community-engagement); BrandChamp guide to Lululemon's ambassador programs (https://brandchamp.io/blog/lululemon-ambassador-program/, secondary); Yahoo/Style, "Run clubs are booming..." (https://sg.style.yahoo.com/run-clubs-booming-central-role-145719902.html, search summary); Modern Retail, brands tapping run clubs for sampling and sponsorship, 2024 (https://www.modernretail.co/marketing/as-running-clubs-explode-in-popularity-brands-are-tapping-these-groups-for-sampling-opportunities-and-sponsorships/, search summary only).
- **Note:** No public source gave a standard per-event fee brands pay independent run clubs. Race sponsorship price lists (for example $3,000 per event in a three-event package) are the closest public proxy. Source: runsignup partner pages in the same search (https://runsignup.com/w/RunGSP/Page/Partnerships). So the $150 number is a judgment call, not a market rate.

### 4. Values gates on sponsors (VegFests)
- **What:** Philly VegFest, Lancaster VegFest and Boston Veg Food Fest require that all food and products be Vegan. VegfestUK London welcomes sponsorship for Vegan products and services and allows some non-Vegan businesses only under a strong Vegan policy.
- **Why it works:** Organizers protect their community's trust with a clear, public rule. Sponsors know the rule before they ask.
- **Lesson for us:** Publish the sponsor rule. Default: fully Vegan brands only at a group's night, with the group's veto on top.
- **Sources:** Lancaster VegFest exhibitor instructions (https://pavegfest.com/vendors/exhibitor-instructions); VegfestUK London criteria (https://london.vegfest.co.uk/criteria/); Philly VegFest 2026 exhibitor and sponsor application (https://docs.google.com/forms/d/e/1FAIpQLSf9ZcacJIqnd4ONa5CRR7k9rTQECms_MQKMMVFL24W3dM4zjQ/viewform); Boston Veg Food Fest sponsor info (https://bostonveg.org/foodfest/sponsor-info/bvff-2022-sponsors-exhibitors).

### 5. Fast money to the host (Luma, Eventbrite)
- **What:** Luma pays organizers through their own Stripe account on Stripe's rolling schedule, typically the next business day, with fees taken per sale. Eventbrite added Instant and Scheduled Payouts before the event for eligible organizers, with a reserve held back.
- **Why it works:** A supper club pays the chef and the venue before the dinner. Holding ticket money until after the event makes the platform unusable for them.
- **Lesson for us:** If we pitch ticketing, pay out before the event (rolling, with a small reserve), or do not lead with ticketing yet. Keep any 30-day hold only for referral shares, never for ticket money.
- **Sources:** Luma Help, Understanding payouts (https://help.luma.com/p/understanding-payouts-and-event-proceeds); Eventbrite Help, Request an instant payout (https://www.eventbrite.com/help/en-us/articles/360444/request-an-instant-payout/); Eventbrite Help, payout schedule (https://www.eventbrite.com/help/en-us/articles/115913/).

### 6. Calendars, featuring and multi-organizer programs (Luma)
- **What:** On Luma every event sits on a calendar that people subscribe to; past attendees are the host's audience. Luma's Tech Weeks put many independent organizers on one shared calendar, each running their own event, with the program admins approving entries. Hosts can apply to be featured.
- **Why it works:** Organizers keep their own events and audience and gain a shared moment and shared promotion. That is exactly the shape of "community nights building toward the Expo."
- **Lesson for us:** Frame the founding nights as one shared season ("Community Nights, Spring 2027") that many clubs are part of, each running its own night. Featuring is earned by joining the season, not by moving events off Meetup.
- **Sources:** Luma Help, Tech Weeks (https://help.luma.com/p/tech-weeks); Luma Help, Featuring your event (https://help.luma.com/p/featuring-your-event-on-luma); Inc. on Luma (https://www.inc.com/rebecca-deczynski/meet-the-seven-person-startup-powering-silicon-valleys-event-boom/91408964, search summary).

### 7. Hosts recruit hosts (Partiful)
- **What:** Partiful grew through hosts sharing invite links; guests who enjoyed a party later became hosts. Its founder calls brand the deeper moat.
- **Why it works:** The best recruiting tool is a great night that a guest wants to throw too.
- **Lesson for us:** Every recap names the club and ends with "Run a night like this with your group." Pay a referral to the club that brings the next club.
- **Sources:** NoGood, Partiful marketing strategy (https://nogood.io/blog/partiful-marketing-strategy/); Partiful 2024 year in review (https://info.partiful.com/blog/post/2024-year-in-review). Both partly secondary.

### 8. Founding cohorts and legacy terms (Patreon)
- **What:** Patreon began with a handful of creators; the visible success of the founder's own page pulled in hundreds more. Early creators kept a "Founders" legacy plan. Patreon's growth team found that improving creator onboarding was its strongest lever, because creator success seeds more creators.
- **Why it works:** Early partners get terms later partners cannot, and the visible success of the first few is the marketing.
- **Lesson for us:** A Founding Host cohort with terms locked for life, closed on a date. Spend effort on making the first three nights great, not on reaching more organizers.
- **Sources:** TechCrunch, the Patreon story (https://techcrunch.com/2019/02/12/patreon-story); Brian Balfour, Patreon onboarding growth (https://brianbalfour.com/essays/patreon-onboarding-growth); Contrary Research on Patreon (https://research.contrary.com/report/patreon).

### 9. Getting money to groups that are not organizations (fiscal sponsorship)
- **What:** Informal groups commonly receive grant money through a fiscal sponsor, or the funder pays a vendor or a named nonprofit directly.
- **Why it works:** A potluck has no bank account and no appetite for paperwork. Paying the venue bill, or a cause the group names, removes the W-9 problem for most small groups.
- **Lesson for us:** Let the club choose where its contribution goes: the club's account, the venue directly, or a nonprofit it names.
- **Sources:** Nonprofit Quarterly, Is fiscal sponsorship right for you (https://nonprofitquarterly.org/is-fiscal-sponsorship-right-for-you/); Idealist, Fiscal sponsorships (https://www.idealist.org/en/action-incubator/fiscal-sponsorships-an-alternative-pathway-to-funding).

### 10. Data norms (general knowledge plus Eventbrite)
- Ticketing platforms share attendee data with the organizer, who controls it under their own policy; Eventbrite widened organizer access to attendee emails in 2026 (ConductAtlas change record, https://conductatlas.com/change/2026-08-21-eventbrite-eventbrite-privacy-policy-4525/, third-party summary). Rival platforms market against Eventbrite on "who owns the list" (https://info.ticketsignup.io/2024/08/28/stop-giving-eventbrite-your-attendee-data/).
- **General knowledge:** organizers judge platforms first on "will you email my people." The strongest promise is a short, specific one that is easy to keep: we never message, export or sell your members' contact details, and sponsors never get them.

### What did not exist publicly
No published standard rate for what brands pay grassroots clubs per night. No source showed a sponsored-night program that cancels after the club announces; the programs that work fund first.

## S: System (how we get there)

### Method: a hand-built pilot, then a standard
1. **Fund first.** Before pitching any organizer, sign sponsor money for the spring season. Pitching a club with no money behind it repeats the current problem.
2. **Pilot cohort of three**, hand-picked from the founder's relationships, in the three planned regions (South Florida, Central Florida with Vegans in Clermont, Philadelphia with Philly Vegan Social Club's first birthday on 2027-03-13).
3. **Run each night with a written checklist** (below). After each night: a 20-minute debrief with the organizer, a short member survey at the night, the numbers written down.
4. **Change one thing at a time** between nights; record it in the playbook.
5. **Publish the proof**: recap, photos, the dollar amount paid, and the organizer's own words.
6. **Open the Founding Host cohort** wider in April with the recaps as the pitch.

### The first 90 days (2026-11-20 to 2027-02-17)

**Weeks 1 and 2 (to 2026-12-03): lock the offer**
- Founder decides the five decisions below.
- Rewrite the Group Partner Guide to the structure in this plan. Remove the share ladder and the "featured comes with hosting here" line.
- Write the one-page Host Agreement (terms card plus cancellation, data and sponsor rules).
- Build the sponsor menu (three formats) and the sponsor agreement: payment due at booking, non-refundable from the 30-day lock.

**Weeks 2 to 4 (to 2026-12-17): fund the season**
- Pitch 15 fully Vegan brands that already sponsor VegFests or sell in the three regions. Ask for a spring season package (three nights, $1,200), single nights ($500), or "In the bag" product.
- Goal: cash for three nights on hand or contracted by 2026-12-17. If cash covers only two, run two. Do not pitch a third club without money for it.

**Weeks 3 to 6 (to 2026-12-31): sign the founding three**
- The founder sends a personal note to 10 organizers he knows (no form, no link first). One call each, 20 minutes, with the Community Manager.
- On the call: show the terms card, ask "which night already on your calendar would you like to make bigger?", agree the date.
- Signed means: the Host Agreement accepted, a date held, the sponsor shortlist sent for their approval.

**Weeks 6 to 9 (to 2027-01-21): build each night**
- Club approves or vetoes each sponsor within 7 days.
- Lock date: 35 days before the night, go or no-go. At 30 days, if funded, the club announces. If not funded at 35 days, we move it with the club before anyone outside knows (the club never announced, so nobody loses face).
- Community Manager confirms venue needs, bag counts, giveaway, photographer.

**Weeks 9 to 13 (to 2027-02-17): first night and learn**
- First night runs mid-February (South Florida or Clermont).
- Contribution paid within 7 days. Recap delivered to the club within 5 days, theirs to post first.
- Debrief, change one thing, run the next.
- Philadelphia's 2027-03-13 birthday night locks on 2027-02-06 and announces 2027-02-11 (falls just after day 90).

| -50 | Sponsor shortlist sent to the club; club approves or vetoes within 7 days |

## S: Simplify (the program, written for a Community Manager)

### Name
**Founding Host** for the first cohort (closes when full or 2027-06-30). **Group Partner** after that. The night is a **Community Night**.

### The offer in one sentence
Pick a night your club already runs; we fund it, bring the Vegan sponsors you approve, do the work, and pay your club $150, and we never announce it until the money is in.

### The terms card (what goes at the top of the guide)
- **$150 to your club for every Community Night** (founder decides the amount; $150 recommended). Paid within 7 days after the night, to the club's account, straight to the venue, or to a nonprofit the club names.
- **Funded before you announce.** Sponsors pay before the 30-day mark. If a night is not funded 35 days out, we move it with you privately, before anyone hears about it. After the 30-day mark the night happens, even if a sponsor pulls out (their payment is already in).
- **You approve every sponsor.** Fully Vegan brands only, and you can say no to any of them, no reason needed.
- **Your members stay yours.** We never message, export or sell your members' details, and sponsors never get them.
- **Keep everything you use now.** Meetup, Partiful, Instagram, your ticketing. Nothing moves.
- **Nothing to pay, nothing to sign up for first.** A 20-minute call and one page.

### What the club gets on a Community Night
- Sponsors at the welcome table, in the format the club picks (see below).
- A giveaway drawn live.
- Thank-you bags.
- A photo wall and a photographer.
- Promotion with the club named first: our calendar, the city's Daily Pulse, our accounts, and paid ads where the sponsor budget allows. Be honest that our reach is small and growing; the money is the main thing we bring, not the audience.
- A recap the club posts first, as its own.
- $150 to the club.

### Sponsor formats (the club picks one per night)
1. **Welcome table:** a sponsor at a table with tastings or samples. Best for social clubs and potlucks.
2. **In the bag:** products in thank-you bags only, no table, no talk. Best for chef dinners and supper clubs, where tastings would compete with the chef.
3. **Quiet thanks:** sponsors named in the recap and thanked once from the front; no logos on site, nothing handed out. Best for screenings and activist nights.

### Sponsor rules (published)
- Fully Vegan brands only at Community Nights. (A Pre-Vegan brand only if the club asks for it in writing.)
- The club can veto any sponsor, at any time before the 30-day lock.
- Sponsors never receive guest contact details. Guests who want a sponsor's offer get it through a code, not a sign-up sheet.
- Sponsors do not speak for more than a sentence at the welcome; the organizer leads the room.

### Sponsor pricing (to the brand)
- In the bag: free (product only).
- One Community Night: $500.
- Season of three nights: $1,200.
(These are the founder's current drafts; I recommend keeping them.)

### Where the money goes on a $500 night (internal, show the club if asked)
- $150 to the club.
- About $150 for the giveaway, bag extras, photo wall and printing (most bag contents are donated product).
- About $100 for the photographer or the Community Manager's gig.
- About $100 for paid local promotion and a reserve (10% of every sponsor payment goes to a small Night Reserve that covers a shortfall, so the founder never pays out of pocket).
Founder decides the exact split. The rule: the club's $150 is paid first.

### If a sponsor drops
- Before the 30-day lock: we replace them or move the date privately with the club. The club has not announced, so nothing is lost.
- After the lock: the sponsor's payment is non-refundable by contract, so the night runs. If product does not arrive, the Night Reserve covers bags.

### Money flows and payout timing
- Club contribution: within 7 days after the night.
- Referral thanks (below): within 7 days after the referred club's first night.
- Tickets (only if the platform can do it): pay out on a rolling basis before the event, like Luma and Eventbrite's pre-event payouts, holding a small reserve. If that is not built, do not pitch ticketing to supper clubs yet; say "Coming."
- Paperwork: ask for a W-9 only when money goes to the club's or organizer's own account, and only at the time of the first payment, never on the intake. Money paid to a venue or to a named nonprofit needs no W-9 from the club. (Founder confirms current 1099 thresholds with the accountant; reporting thresholds changed recently, general knowledge.)

### Data and member-list promises (exact words)
- "We never message, export or sell your members' contact details."
- "Sponsors never get your members' details."
- "People who join Vegans Explore at your night hear from us only because they signed up themselves, and they can stop any time."
- "If your organizer changes, your page and history stay with the club."
Nothing more. Do not promise a member export or an organizer dashboard until they are built; say "Coming."

### Activist and nonprofit groups: Cause Nights
Same terms card, with three differences:
- **The contribution funds an action the group already runs:** screening rights, outreach materials, a sanctuary's feed bill. Paid to the group or straight to the cost.
- **Quiet thanks only:** no logos at outreach, no tastings at screenings, sponsors fully Vegan.
- **No-sponsor option:** a group can choose a night funded from the season pool (several brands paying into one pool) with no brand named on site at all, only in the recap thanks.
Dani, the Causes Guide, walks this part.

### The application: drop it for invited groups
- Organizers we reach out to get a call, not a form.
- Organizers who find us on their own get a three-question form: the group and its link, the night you would like to make bigger, how to reach you. Then a call.
- The five-step form goes. Nonprofit status, member counts and "the fit" are asked on the call if they matter.

| Sponsors chosen by us | Change: club veto, fully Vegan only, three formats | Stopper for four |
| Share ladder (20% to 50%) | Drop from the guide | Pays nothing today; reads like a funnel (five of five) |
| "Featured status comes with hosting your events here" | Drop | Reads as the catch; feature every partner |
| Five-step application | Drop for invited groups; three questions for inbound | Felt like an audition |
| Nonprofit and W-9 questions on intake | Drop from intake; ask at first payment only | Too official for a potluck |
| 30-day hold on ticket payouts | Change for tickets: pay before the event, or do not pitch ticketing yet | Kills supper clubs |
| Founding Membership for the organizer | Keep, as a small thank-you, not a selling point | Nice; not why anyone says yes |
| Our ads behind groups | Keep, honestly sized | Real, but our reach is small |
| Bounties first pick | Drop from this guide | Not relevant to the organizer's decision; adds noise |
| Ticketing 5% comparison | Keep, in its own part, only for ticketed groups | Landed with the ticketed groups |
| Organizer dashboard demo | Keep as "Coming"; never lead with it | Not built; four asked about it |

### Show, do not tell: the first three nights as a proof campaign
- **Before the nights:** the guide shows the plan for a night, a sample run sheet, the terms card and the sponsor rule. No photos pretending to be real; the sample club stays stamped Sample.
- **Each night:** a photographer, a 60-second recap video, and the organizer's own words on camera (with consent).
- **After each night:** a recap page per night: the club, the date, the turnout, the sponsors, "$150 paid to the club on [date]," and the organizer's quote. The club posts it first.
- **After the third night:** a short "Spring Community Nights" story with all three clubs. This becomes the first part of the guide and the attachment to every new invite.
- **The pitch to the next wave** is not the guide. It is: "Elle in Clermont ran one. Here is what happened. Want to talk to her?" With her permission, organizers can call a Founding Host.

### Creating the line down the block
1. **A cohort with an end:** Founding Host closes when it is full or on 2027-06-30. Founding Hosts keep their terms for life (the $150, the veto, the data promise) even if later terms change. (Founder decides the cohort size; I recommend 12 groups. A count of groups is fine; do not put counts of chapters or cities in copy.)
2. **A season everyone is part of:** "Spring Community Nights." Each night is its own; the season is shared. Every recap links to the others.
3. **A Host Circle:** Founding Hosts meet once a month online for 45 minutes with the founder. They see the sponsor list first, shape the rules, and are first in line for community nights around the World Vegan Expo. Organizers want a seat at the table more than a badge.
4. **Organizer to organizer:** when a club brings another club that runs a night, the referring club gets $50 more (funded from the sponsor money, paid within 7 days of that night). Founder decides the amount.
5. **A waitlist outside the first regions:** "Want a Community Night in your city? Hold a spot." When a sponsor covers a region, waitlisted clubs there are called first.
6. **Public proof, never inflated:** real turnout, real money paid, real quotes. In this community, one honest recap beats any claim.

## The founder's decisions (one at a time, each with a recommendation)

### 1. Fund before announcing (the most important)
**Recommendation:** Sponsors pay at booking; a night is go or no-go at 35 days; the club announces only at 30 days, after the money is in; sponsor payment is non-refundable after the lock; 10% of every sponsor payment goes into a Night Reserve.
**Why:** It keeps the founder's rule (he never pays out of pocket) and removes the club's biggest fear (being embarrassed after announcing). Four of five organizers asked for exactly this.
**Default if no override:** this rule goes in the Host Agreement and the sponsor agreement.

### 2. The contribution: $150 per night, stated on the page
**Recommendation:** $150 flat, paid within 7 days after the night, to the club, the venue or a nonprofit the club names.
**Why:** All five organizers stopped on the missing number. $150 is meaningful to a potluck or a screening, covers a typical small venue fee or a film license, fits inside a $500 sponsor night, and is small enough not to look like buying a club. Flat is simpler and fairer than tying it to turnout.
**Default:** $150.

### 3. Sponsor control: fully Vegan only, club veto, three formats
**Recommendation:** Yes to all three.
**Why:** It is the VegFest norm, it protects the club's trust, and it opens the door to supper clubs and activist groups (In the bag, Quiet thanks). Cost: a smaller pool of brands willing to pay cash. Mitigate with the season package and the pooled Cause Night fund.
**Default:** fully Vegan only, Pre-Vegan brands only when a club asks in writing.

### 4. Cut the funnel: drop the share ladder, the "featured if you host here" line, and the application for invited groups
**Recommendation:** Remove all three from the guide now. Bring a referral share back later as one flat rate, once commissions resume and it pays.
**Why:** Each one reads as the platform's ask, not the club's benefit. Five of five flagged the ladder.
**Default:** removed in the guide rewrite.

### 5. Ticketing: pay before the event, or do not pitch it yet
**Recommendation:** For ticket sales, rolling payouts before the event with a small reserve (the Luma and Eventbrite model). If the platform cannot do that by the first nights, take ticketing out of the pitch and say "Coming."
**Why:** Supper clubs pay chefs before dinner. A 30-day hold is a hard no for them, and ticketing is not needed to make the first three nights a success.
**Default:** ticketing is not part of the first-wave pitch until pre-event payouts are confirmed live.

(Also for the founder, lower stakes: the name "Founding Host," the cohort size of 12, the $50 referral, the Host Circle. My recommendations stand as written above.)

## What the Group Partner Guide page should say (section by section)

Built on the locked Guide frame and the Handbook template, walked by Dani. Seven parts, so it fits the existing top menu. Every claim must be true on the day it goes live; anything not built says "Coming."

1. **The deal** (overview). Headline: "Your night, funded. Your club, in charge." The terms card, word for word, at the top. One action: "Pick a night with us." Second link: "Talk to a host who has done it" (only once a night has run).
2. **Your night.** What happens on a Community Night, in five tiles (sponsors, giveaway, bags and photo wall, promotion, recap), then the run sheet from day -60 to day +10 as a simple timeline. Line: "We never announce until the money is in."
3. **Your sponsors, your call.** The sponsor rule (fully Vegan only, your veto), the three formats with who each suits, and "sponsors never get your members' details."
4. **Your members stay yours.** The four data promises, plain. "Keep Meetup, Partiful, Instagram. Nothing moves."
5. **Cause Nights.** For activist groups, sanctuaries and nonprofits: the contribution funds an action you already run, Quiet thanks only, a no-sponsor option. Dani speaks most here.
6. **The proof.** Before February: the plan for the first nights, honestly labeled as planned. After each night: the recap, the turnout, "$150 paid," the organizer's words. Then a small Sample part: the sample club page for what members see; the organizer dashboard marked Coming. Ticketing (5% and the fee comparison) sits here only if pre-event payouts are live.
7. **Pick a night.** Three questions (group and link, the night, how to reach you) or book a 20-minute call. Below it, short answers: Does it cost anything? (No.) Who runs the night? (You.) What if a sponsor drops? (The lock rule.) What if our organizer changes? (The club is the partner.) Do we need to be a nonprofit? (No.)

Words to drop everywhere: Apply, application, reviewed, the fit, featured with hosting, share ladder, bounties.
Tone: plain, short, us committing first. Never claim reach we do not have; say "you are the draw, we bring the funding and the work."

## Risks and how to handle them

| Risk | Likelihood | What we do |
|---|---|---|
| Not enough cash from fully Vegan brands | High | Fund before pitching; season packages; pool brands for Cause Nights; run two nights, not three, if cash covers two |
| A first night goes badly in a tight-knit scene | Medium | Pick strong, warm organizers for the first three; the founder attends; the run sheet; under-promise turnout |
| Looks like buying clubs, or using them for sign-ups | Medium | Club money, not personal; flat and public; no member-sign-up push at the night beyond a QR code people choose |
| Activist backlash at commercial sponsorship | Medium | Quiet thanks, no-sponsor option, fully Vegan only, the group's veto |
| Promising what is not built (dashboard, exports, ticket payouts) | Medium | Every claim checked against the live site before the guide ships; "Coming" otherwise |
| Paperwork and tax confusion | Low to medium | Pay venue or a named nonprofit when the club prefers; W-9 only at first payment; accountant confirms 1099 rules |
| Community Manager capacity across several nights at once | Medium | No more than one night per Community Manager per two weeks in the pilot; the run sheet as a checklist |
| Depending on the founder's relationships | High early, falls later | The first three are personal by design; the recaps and the organizer referral carry the next wave |
| Sponsors push for guest data or more airtime | Medium | Written in the sponsor agreement; codes instead of sign-up sheets; one sentence at the welcome |
| Holiday timing slows sponsor decisions in December | Medium (general knowledge: many brand budgets reset in January) | Ask brands for a spring commitment in early December; let payment fall in January before the 35-day check |
