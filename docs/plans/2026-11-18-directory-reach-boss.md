**Assumptions to confirm:** "reached" means the business was told, by any channel, not that they replied; a tag or mention that sends
them an Instagram notification counts as reached; Thanksgiving Thursday and Friday send nothing.

## B: Best practice (what the evidence says)

1. **Mailbox providers judge the sender on complaints and bounces.** Gmail and Yahoo's bulk-sender rules (in force since 2024) require
   SPF/DKIM/DMARC, one-click unsubscribe honored within two days, and spam complaints under 0.3% (Google's target under 0.1%)
   ([Close](https://help.close.com/docs/requirements-for-bulk-email-senders), [OneSignal](https://documentation.onesignal.com/docs/new-google-and-yahoo-email-updates),
   [Bird](https://bird.com/explained/deliverability/what-are-the-gmail-and-yahoo-bulk-sender-requirements)). Bounces are not in the rule,
   but the industry line is under 2%: providers throttle above about 3% and blocklisting risk rises above 5%
   ([Instantly](https://instantly.ai/blog/email-verification/), [Prospeo](https://prospeo.io/s/email-address-verification)).
   Our sender already paused itself twice on Monday for bounces.
2. **Verify before every send.** The standard pipeline is format check, domain (MX) check, then a mailbox probe; catch-all domains are
   kept in a separate pile and pulled if they bounce. Services charge about $3 to $8 per 1,000 (vendor pages, to confirm before buying:
   [NeverBounce pricing](https://www.activepieces.com/blog/neverbounce-pricing-in-2026-credits-plans-costs),
   [ZeroBounce vs NeverBounce](https://bouncezero.io/zerobounce-vs-neverbounce)). Our whole list is under 2,000 addresses: under $20.
3. **A second channel beats more volume on one.** Vendor benchmarks (not controlled studies) agree on direction: cold email alone gets
   1 to 5% replies; pairing it with a social touch lifts replies, with 2 to 3 days between touches and 3 to 5 social touches over two
   to three weeks ([Reply.io](https://reply.io/reply-multichannel-outreach/), [Apollo](https://www.apollo.io/insights/what-are-the-benefits-of-multi-channel-outreach-email-calls-social-media),
   [ColdIQ case](https://somethinginc.com/blog/coldiq-multichannel-reply-rate-case-study/), [Prospeo](https://prospeo.io/s/social-sales-cadence)).
4. **Video on the landing page helps people act.** Vendor case studies report longer time on page and higher conversion when a short
   video leads the page (Vidyard on Act-On, Unbounce test), self-reported, so treat as direction, not a number
   ([Unbounce/Vidyard](https://www.casestudies.com/company/unbounce/case-study/using-video-to-lift-landing-page-conversion-rate-by-100),
   [Wistia thumbnails](https://wistia.com/blog/testing-video-thumbnails-in-emails)).
5. **Instagram DMs: slow, personal, follow first.** Meta publishes no limits and bans spam however it is sent. Our own research
   (Positions BOSS, 2026-11-13): 20 new DMs a day, 3 an hour, follow first; a DM to someone who follows us lands in their main inbox,
   otherwise in Requests. No third-party tool can start a DM.
6. **One message, versioned, beats many campaigns.** (Our own lesson: copy drifted across the holiday campaigns; canon
   `canon-sean-one-next-step` and Sean's "outreach is the last step" rule: the page and offer are locked before anything sends.)

## S: System (how we get there)

**PDCA in daily loops, with a two-day pilot first.** Plan (groups, pages, messages), Do (one day of sends per group), Check (bounces,
visits, replies, KJ's notes), Act (edit the group's living message for the next day). It suits a fast-changing offer: every day is a
small, measured test, and the message improves while it runs.

**The four lanes, so every listing has a way in:**

| Lane | Who | How many | How |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Email | Logan's system | 287 now, plus what Lane 3 finds | Checked address only, the group's current email, tracked link to the group page |
| 2. Instagram | **KJ** | 994 (follow-first), top priority first | Her dashboard queue: follow, look, then DM when the cap allows; personal tracked link |
| 2b. Instagram tags | Logan drafts, Sean approves | the rest of the 994 KJ cannot DM in time | City "You're in the Directory" posts and stories that tag the listings (Instagram notifies each tagged account); KJ DMs the ones who engage |
| 3. Find them | Logan's system (Apify, now working) | 1,528 website only | Read each website for an email or contact form; found emails go through the check, then Lane 1; contact forms become cards |
| 4. Find them later | Logan | 172 with nothing | Kept on a list, not part of December 1 |

## S: Simplify (make it repeatable)

- **One rule for every list: no address is sent to until it passes the check.** The check runs by itself on any new contact.
- **One table, one status per listing**: group, lane, version sent, reached at, result. Every board reads from it; KJ's queue is a view
  of it.
- **One living message per group and lane.** Changing the offer means editing four messages, not hunting through campaigns.
- **The playbook**: this file becomes the SOP for any later push (a new chapter, a new offer): count the lanes, check the lists, lock
  the pages, run the daily loop.

## Continuous improvement loop

- Daily: bounces, visits, claims, replies per group; KJ's notes ("looks closed" goes to Directory review); edit the living message if a
  version underperforms.
- After the pilot (two days): set real targets from real numbers; decide whether Lane 2b (tag posts) is needed and how many.
- December 2: a one-page result: reached by lane and group, claims, what to change before the January 1 push.
