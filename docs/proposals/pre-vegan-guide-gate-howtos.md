## G1 Ingredient evidence

**The rule.** Every ingredient and sub-ingredient in the dish is traced to a brand and product. A distributor's say-so alone does not
count. Missing evidence fails.

**What to do**
1. Write the recipe card for the dish: every ingredient, by brand and product, in the amounts you use.
2. For each thing you buy, photograph the front of the package and the ingredient list. If it comes in a case with no retail label,
   ask the supplier for the specification sheet.
3. Read every label down to the sub-ingredients (what is inside the sauce, the bread, the spice blend).
4. Take out anything with honey, beeswax, carmine (also called cochineal or E120), shellac (confectioner's glaze), gelatin,
   isinglass or animal rennet, and anything whose label says "Contains milk" or "Contains egg". Swap the brand, or leave that dish
   out.
5. For these ten, ask the maker in writing and keep the answer: natural flavors, wine, beer, vinegar, vitamin D3, cane sugar,
   enzymes, glycerin, emulsifiers (mono and diglycerides, lecithin from an unnamed source) and anything fermented. A short email
   works: "Is [product] free of all animal-derived ingredients and processing aids, including [the item]? Please reply in writing."
6. "May contain milk" or a shared-facility line is not a fail. Note it; it goes in the dish's disclosure.

**How to prove it.** One folder per dish: the recipe card, a label photo or spec sheet for every bought ingredient, and the written
answers from step 5.

**What the Verifier checks.** At the desk: every ingredient named by brand and product, a label or spec sheet for each, no hard-stop
ingredient, every item on the ten-item list answered in writing. In the kitchen: what is on the shelf matches the folder, three
invoices pulled at random match the brands, every unlabeled container is identified, and each packaged ingredient is scanned.

**Common ways kitchens fail.** A vegetable stock with no brand named. A bun from a supplier with no label photo. A house dressing with
honey.

## G2 Hidden in process

**The rule.** Anything the dish touches on its way to the plate (stocks, doughs, glazes, pan spray, frying oil, shared bases) is traced
the same way as an ingredient.

**What to do**
1. Walk the dish from the shelf to the plate and write down every base it uses: stock, dough, bread or bun, glaze, sauce, dressing,
   marinade, pan release spray, frying or cooking oil, butter substitute, garnish, topping.
2. For anything you make in house, write its own recipe card and trace its ingredients as in G1.
3. Check the sprays and oils most kitchens forget: many pan sprays contain lecithin or flavorings, and "butter-flavored" oils can
   contain milk.
4. If a base is shared with other dishes (a house sauce, a pizza dough), it must pass for this dish too, or this dish gets its own.

**How to prove it.** A one-page process map for each dish: every base it uses, each with its trace attached.

**What the Verifier checks.** The map against what they watch being made. A base used at the station that is not on the map fails the
dish until it is traced.

**Common ways kitchens fail.** Butter brushed on the bun at the pass. A shared stock nobody thought of as an ingredient.

## G3 Protected path

**The rule.** Every certified dish has a protected cooking path: its own storage, utensils and surfaces, or a clean separate pan, pot
or barrier. Never shared fryer oil, shared cooking water, or a grill used for animal products and not cleaned first. A shared oven only
under a written procedure we approved.

**What to do**
1. **Storage.** Give the dish's ingredients their own shelf or bin, labeled, never under raw animal products unless covered.
2. **Tools.** Set aside a pan, pot, tongs, ladle and cutting board for the dish (a color you use for nothing else works well). Or use
   a pan that is cleaned and sanitized and holds only this dish during its cook.
3. **Surfaces.** A clean sheet, foil or a dedicated tray between the dish and any shared surface.
4. **Fryer.** A dedicated fryer, or no frying for this dish. Shared oil with chicken or fish is a fail, no matter how often it is
   changed.
5. **Water.** Fresh water for pasta or vegetables, never the pot that cooked anything else.
6. **Grill or flat-top.** Scrape and clean it before the dish goes on, every time, or use a barrier or a separate pan on top.
7. **Oven.** Covered or on its own tray, and write down how you do it. Send that procedure with your evidence.
8. **Garnish.** A separate garnish container for the dish, not the shared station with cheese, butter or bacon.

| Passes | Fails |
|---|---|
| Dedicated pan, pot, utensils and board | Shared fryer oil with any animal product |
| A shared pan, cleaned and sanitized, used only for this dish during its cook | Pasta or vegetable water shared with animal products |
| A clean barrier on a shared surface | A grill used for meat and not cleaned first |
| Shared oven, covered or own tray, under a written procedure | Shared oven, no procedure |
| | Tongs or ladles moving between the dish and animal products |
| | A shared garnish station with no separation |
| | Stored under raw animal products, uncovered |

**How to prove it.** Where each dish is stored, prepped and cooked, a photo of each station, and the written oven procedure if you use
a shared oven.

**What the Verifier checks.** They watch each dish made start to finish by the person who usually makes it, at normal service pace, and
photograph each station. If they see something the table does not cover, they write it down and the approver decides; they never rule
at the counter.

**Common ways kitchens fail.** Fries in the shared fryer. Pasta in the shared water. The same tongs for the chicken and the vegetables
during a rush.

## G4 Accurate use

**The rule.** The mark and the dish names are accurate everywhere the dish appears, delivery apps included.

**What to do**
1. List every place the dish appears: printed menu, boards, website, QR menu, each delivery app, your social accounts.
2. Use the same dish name everywhere, word for word.
3. Put the mark only next to certified dishes, with the menu key ("Plant-Based Dish, Pre-Vegan Certified") on the same page or screen.
4. Never call the restaurant Vegan, never put the mark on the door or the whole menu, never write "Vegan" alone on a certified dish, and
   never "allergen-free", "100% safe" or "inspected".
5. On delivery apps, ask each platform to change a dish called "Vegan" to the certified name with the key in the description.

**How to prove it.** A screenshot or photo of every place the dish appears.

**What the Verifier checks.** The printed menu, boards, website and every delivery app that lists the dish, against your list.

**Common ways kitchens fail.** A delivery app still listing the dish as "Vegan" with no key.

## G5 Staff

**The rule.** Every critical staff question is answered right. One manager is named for these dishes, and there is a training roster.

**What to do**
1. Name one manager who is responsible for the certified dishes.
2. Train everyone who takes orders for or cooks these dishes. Write down who and the date. Retrain anyone new before their first shift
   on these dishes.
3. Teach these answers. The Verifier asks two staff members questions like these, and one wrong answer fails the visit:
   - Is this dish Vegan? *It is 100% plant-based, prepared on a protected path, and certified for this dish only. Here is the status
     page.*
   - Can you add cheese (or butter, or egg) to it? *Yes, but then it is no longer the certified dish, and we will tell you so.*
   - Is the rest of the menu Vegan? *No. Only the marked dishes are certified.*
   - Is it allergen-free? *No. It has no animal ingredients, but we do not certify allergens. Here is what is in it.*
   - Is it cooked in the same fryer, water or pan as meat? *No. It has its own path.* (They should be able to say what that path is.)
   - What if we are out of an ingredient? *We do not swap it. We take the dish off until we have the approved one.*
   - Who do I ask if I have a question? *The manager on duty, [name].*

   (Draft wording. These must match the seven critical questions on Form B before this page goes live.)
4. Post the script where order-takers can see it.

**How to prove it.** The manager's name, the training roster with dates, and the order-taking script.

**What the Verifier checks.** At least two staff members who make or serve the dishes, the critical questions, and the roster. Secret
shoppers ask "Is this Vegan?" between visits.

**Common ways kitchens fail.** A server who says "it has a little butter". A new hire who was never trained.

## G6 Change approval

**The rule.** Any change to a certified dish is approved by us before it is served. Otherwise the mark stops at once and the change is
reported.

**What to do**
1. Write an approved pantry list: for each ingredient, the brands you are allowed to use.
2. Check every delivery against the list when it arrives. A different brand goes to the side, not into the dish.
3. Ask your suppliers in writing not to substitute on these items without telling you.
4. Before you change a recipe, a brand, a base or the way the dish is cooked, tell us and wait for the yes. A change we did not approve
   means the dish's mark stops until it is checked again. Hiding a change ends every certification at the location and removes the
   restaurant from Vegans Explore for 2 years.
5. If you are out of an approved ingredient, take the dish off rather than swap it.

**How to prove it.** The approved pantry list, and a signed promise to ask before any change is served.

**What the Verifier checks.** The pantry list against the shelf and three invoices, and anything on the shelf not on the list.

**Common ways kitchens fail.** A distributor substitutes a different stock and nobody checks the delivery.

## G7 Access and corrective action

**The rule.** Our visits and your records are open to us. Suspension does not wait on proof of intent; intent only sets the penalty.
An independent appeal: 10 business days to appeal, a decision within 20 business days of a complete record.

**What to do**
1. Agree to a scheduled kitchen visit, one courtesy check a year, and secret shoppers who order like any guest.
2. Agree to photos of storage, stations and labels (never audio, never guests).
3. Know what happens if something is wrong: the dish's mark stops while it is fixed, you can appeal within 10 business days, and you
   get a decision within 20 business days of a complete record.

**How to prove it.** Signed consent to the visit, the yearly check, records review and photos.

**What the Verifier checks.** That they were let in, saw what they asked to see, and could photograph it.

## G8 Records

**The rule.** Records are kept for the term plus 5 years.

**What to do**
1. Keep, in one place, every receiving label and invoice for these dishes' ingredients, the written answers from makers, the training
   roster and every change approval.
2. Name the person who keeps them.
3. Keep them for the 12-month term and 5 years after. A shared drive folder per dish is enough.

**How to prove it.** Where the records are kept and who keeps them.

**What the Verifier checks.** Three invoices pulled at random, and that the folder holds what this page lists.

## Measuring Guide adoption (proposal)

**What counts as adoption**: a restaurant's readiness moves toward ready, whether or not it ever pays. Readiness is private (only the
member reads their own), so the team sees **counts only**, never a named restaurant's score (Logan's default, Sean's call).

**What is missing today.** `ve_preveg_readiness(member)` scores the answers as they stand now. Survey answers are overwritten when
changed, so there is no "before". We need a history.

**Build (when approved)**
1. `ve_preveg_readiness_snaps` (member, at, overall_pct, gates jsonb, blockers int, ready bool; RLS on, no policies): one row when the
   assessment is first finished, then one each time an answer to it changes (a trigger on `ve_survey_answers` for this survey, at most one
   row a member a day).
2. Optional self-report: each "What to do" step gets a tick ("Done in my kitchen"), saved like the Pre-Vegan Business Guide's checklist
   (a `ve-cookbook` save, kind `fix`, key `<gate>-<step>`). Ticks are a second signal; they never change the score.
3. `ve_preveg_adoption(from, to)` (service role) returns, for the period:
   - **Started**: finished the assessment.
   - **Came back**: changed at least one answer after the first finish.
   - **Moved up**: latest overall score higher than the first.
   - **Gates improved**: count per gate of members whose gate score rose, and how many cleared a blocker.
   - **Reached ready**: first score not ready, latest ready (no blocker, every gate at 75% or more).
   - **Ready without applying**: reached ready and no certificate or application (the v9 success number).
   - **Applied / certified**: for comparison, once paid applications open.
   - Median days from first finish to ready.
   - Step ticks by gate (which how-tos get used).
4. A card in Depot > Pre-Vegan Certification, **Guide adoption**, with those numbers for 30 / 90 days / all time. No names.

**Headline number for Sean**: *Ready without applying*, beside *Moved up*. Paid certifications are reported next to them, never instead.
