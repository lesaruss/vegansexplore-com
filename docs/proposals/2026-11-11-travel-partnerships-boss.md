# BOSS: Vegans Explore Travel, set up right from the start (2026-11-11)

Sean: "Before we start running down this path, I want us to... run a boss on what's the best practice... We want to be the source for
people finding retreats, finding vegan-friendly hotels, and we want to build the relationships with those organizations so they can tap
into what we're doing. We also want to use this as leverage for the work we're going to be doing with the tourism bureaus... to have this
whole infrastructure could send a great message to them that there's someone they should invest in." A panel follows this BOSS.

## Step 0: Define the challenge

We are not choosing an affiliate network. We are building a travel source that three groups trust at once:

1. **Travelers** need to believe the list is honest: every place earned its spot, and a paid link is labelled.
2. **Travel businesses** (Vegan hotels, retreats, cruise operators) need us to send them guests in a way that helps them, so they want a
   relationship with us.
3. **Tourism bureaus** need proof: an audience that travels, the businesses in their destination, and data that shows visitors' interest.

The risk is that the money shapes the list, or that the way we earn hurts the businesses we want as partners. Either one breaks the trust
that makes us worth investing in. Root question: in what order do we send a traveler to book, and what do we stand for publicly?

Assumptions to test: that Vegan travelers prefer a trusted, checked list over the big booking sites; that small Vegan hotels will give us a
better deal than they give the big booking sites; that a tourism bureau will pay for a Vegan audience and data.

## B: Best practice

1. **Selection that cannot be bought (the Michelin model).** The Michelin Guide added hotels in 2024 (the Michelin Key, 5,000+ hotels in 120
   countries). Its rule: inspectors are anonymous and independent, a "watertight seal" separates sales from selection, a hotel cannot pay to
   be listed, and a hotel does not need to be bookable through Michelin to earn a Key. Michelin earns a commission (reported 10 to 15%) only
   when a guest books through it. Why it works: the list is the product; booking money comes after, from trust. Sources: Michelin's
   [hotelier guide](https://guide.michelin.com/en/everything-to-know-hoteliers-hotel-selection),
   [The Peak](https://www.thepeakmagazine.com.sg/lifestyle/michelin-guide-hotels-key-selection),
   [The Washington Post via HANYC](https://hanyc.org/the-washington-post-michelin-guide-to-add-hotel-key-ratings-in-2024/). A 2025 critique
   ([The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/lifestyle/travel/2025/10/23/michelin-wants-us-to-trust-its-hotel-ratings-but-should-we)) notes
   tourism boards have paid for Michelin's restaurant inspections: being open about who pays is part of trust.
2. **What booking sites cost the hotels we want as partners.** Hotels pay booking sites about 15 to 25% of each booking (Booking.com about
   15 to 18%, Expedia about 18 to 22%, more for visibility programs); a booking on the hotel's own site costs it roughly 5 to 12%. Small
   hotels work hard to win direct bookings. ([Hospitality Net, 2026 review](https://www.hospitalitynet.org/opinion/4133893/global-hospitality-industry-review-first-half-of-2026),
   [RateGain](https://rategain.com/blog/hotel-direct-booking-vs-ota/), [Hotel Online](https://www.hotel-online.com/news/what-otas-actually-cost-in-2026)).
   So every guest we send through Booking.com costs our hotel about 15% while we earn about 4%. A direct link costs them far less, which is
   the opening for a real relationship.
3. **Disclosure the law expects (FTC Endorsement Guides, updated 2023).** A paid link needs a disclosure that is hard to miss and easy to
   understand, placed before or right beside the link; "affiliate link" or a buy button alone is not enough, "paid link" next to the link
   likely is; a footer or a separate disclosure page does not count. ([InfoLawGroup](https://infolawgroup.com/insights/2023/7/31/ftc-releases-updated-endorsement-guides-10-key-takeaways),
   [Manatt](https://manatt.com/insights/newsletters/advertising-law/an-in-depth-look-at-the-ftcs-updates-2); primary source 16 CFR Part 255.)
   Google asks for `rel="sponsored"` on paid links (already on our Book button).
4. **"Book with us" is selling travel, which Florida licenses.** Florida's Seller of Travel law covers anyone selling travel; an advisor
   working under a host agency files as an Independent Agent (about $50 a year) under the host's registration and bond; selling without it
   risks fines up to $5,000 and a cease and desist ([Fla. Stat. 559.928](https://www.flrules.org/gateway/statute.asp?id=559.928),
   [Travel Market Report](https://www.travelmarketreport.com/articles/What-Travel-Advisors-Need-to-Know-about-the-Florida-Seller-of-Travel-License),
   [Fora](https://www.foratravel.com/the-journal/how-to-become-a-travel-agent-in-florida)). Referral and affiliate links are not selling
   travel; taking bookings as an agency is. Check current FDACS guidance before choosing a host.
5. **What tourism bureaus fund.** Bureaus are mostly paid for by hotel and restaurant taxes and are under pressure to prove return. They
   look for partners who bring an audience, a bookable product and a path to booking, and they measure clicks to local businesses and
   attributed sign-ups; the better ones build a small "bench" of trusted long-term partners instead of one-off trips
   ([Track360](https://track360.io/blog/destination-marketing-organization-partnerships-operator-guide-2026),
   [DCI](https://aboutdci.com/blog/partners-not-posts-4-ways-dmos-elevate-influencers-beyond-pretty-pictures/),
   [Sojern 2026](https://sojern.com/blog/inside-the-state-of-destination-marketing-2026-what-every-dmo-needs-to-know)). The clearest Vegan
   precedent: the Aruba Tourism Authority supported a Vegan Aruba campaign of hosted influencer and press trips (flights from the bureau,
   rooms and meals from local businesses) ([Sandra Nomoto interview, 2022](https://www.sandranomoto.com/2022/11/23/vegan-marketing-success-stories-contributor-meredith-marin-vegan-hospitality/),
   [Aruba.com](https://aruba.com/us/news/the-sunset-grille-delights-vegan-social-media-influencers-with-a-special-dining-experience)).
   Corrected by the panel: HappyCow's 2019 claim was the most Vegan listings per capita in the Caribbean, not "most Vegan-friendly island"
   ([HappyCow](https://www.happycow.net/blog/?p=21810)). State it exactly in any pitch.
6. **The model to avoid.** HappyCow sells restaurants "top placement" on its city pages ([HappyCow](https://happycow.net/supporter)). Paid
   placement in the ranking is the opposite of the Michelin rule and would undercut our "Vegans Explore approved" suitcase mark.

## O: Outcome (what success looks like, by 2027-03-31)

- **Trust**: 100% of Travel listings checked on their own site (in place today); 0 paid placements in any ranking; 100% of paid links
  labelled at the button.
- **Relationships**: 10 stays, retreats or cruise operators with a direct partner agreement (a Vegans Explore member rate or code on their
  own booking site, and a referral fee below what booking sites charge); every Travel listing invited to claim its free page.
- **Data**: a monthly report of Travel clicks by destination and by listing from our tracked `/go/` links, ready to show a bureau.
- **Tourism bureaus**: one pilot destination package and one bureau meeting with that report.
- **Revenue**: tracked, not targeted, in this phase.

## S: System (PDCA, one pilot destination)

**Plan.** Adopt the standard below. Pick one pilot destination where we have a chapter and a bureau to talk to (South Florida is home; its
Vegan stays, retreats, restaurants and events in one place is the package a bureau understands).
**Do.** (1) Fix the Book card: "Paid link" label at the button, and the booking order below. (2) Invite each Travel listing to claim its
page and offer a direct partner agreement. (3) Switch on Booking.com links only where a stay has no direct deal. (4) Build the pilot
destination's package and the monthly data report.
**Check.** Monthly: clicks by destination, partner agreements signed, claims, any complaint about a paid link.
**Act.** Widen to the next destination, or change the order or terms from what partners and the data say.

**Later, not now:** "Book with us" as an agency through a host agency (Florida Seller of Travel covered by the host) for cruises, retreats
and group trips; a Vegan-friendly stay standard modeled on the Pre-Vegan Certification (never promised until built).

## S: Simplify (the standard)

**Listed on merit. Booked the way that is best for the place. Every paid link labelled. Data for destinations.**

The booking order on every Travel listing:
1. The business's own booking site with our partner code (a direct partner).
2. The business's own website.
3. A booking site link (Booking.com first), labelled "Paid link", only when there is no direct option.

Never: paid placement in a list, a site-wide affiliate script, a listing chosen or ranked by what it pays.

## What this changes in the Booking.com-first setup

The card Logan built stays, with two changes before anything goes live: a "Paid link" label at the button (FTC), and the order above, so a
hotel's own booking site wins over Booking.com. Booking.com becomes the fallback, not the plan; it is still worth connecting.

## Conclusion

Recommendation: build Vegans Explore Travel on the Michelin rule (selection can't be bought) with a direct-first booking order, labelled
paid links, and destination data, piloted in one destination for a tourism bureau. Confidence: high on trust and disclosure (published law
and a proven model); medium on how many small Vegan hotels will sign direct agreements and what a bureau will pay, which the pilot answers.
