## Points are coupons (replaces "up to half the price", Earn your Passport part 7, once locked)
- 1 point = 1 cent. On digital items and services we do not physically make (Opportunities packages and ads, select event tickets, Guides, audits, BrandDesk), the buyer types how many points to apply, up to a per-item limit we set, and sees the total fall. Free field, no fixed amounts. "Use up to 10,000 points to bring this to $40."
- No points, no box. Fewer points than the limit: they apply what they have. Outside points they pay the price. A Guide is the same rule with the limit at the full price ($11 or 1,100 points).
- Never on physical goods, contributions or the $11 admission.
- **Events (Sean's answer)**: only select events we run ourselves (community nights, World Vegan Expo). The list price carries the headroom: if $30 sustains the operation, sell at $35 or $40 and allow up to $10 off in points, so the points discount never costs us. The same headroom gives room for affiliates and for nonprofit fundraiser links, which are available to partners only. Group-hosted tickets are not in scope.
- Logan's defaults (Sean's to change): the limit is set per item; points come off at payment and return on an abandoned checkout or refund; spending never lowers a level; commission is on the cash part only; a fully covered total skips Stripe (points-only, as a Guide unlock); the money-in email shows cash and points applied.
- Open: how a nonprofit fundraiser link splits the ticket (who funds the buyer's discount, what share the nonprofit gets).
- Until built: no page, email or Guide promises points toward services or ads.
