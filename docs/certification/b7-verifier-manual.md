## Part 1. The job

Why "Verifier" and not "auditor": an audit sounds like we went through the whole restaurant, its kitchen and its books. We check named
dishes only, and the word says so.

- You are a trained contractor, paid per visit. You are never Vegans Explore staff.
- You check **named dishes** at **one location** against **eight gates**. Every gate must pass. You never certify a restaurant.
- Three different people touch every file: the desk reviewer (evidence), the Verifier (visit), the approver (decision). If you advised
  this restaurant in the last 24 months, or know the owner personally, say so and step off the file.
- You are paid per visit. There is no bonus for a pass. Accept no free meals, drinks, gifts or rides. If staff offer food, say "Thank you,
  our rules don't allow it" and note it on Form B.
- You record what you see. You do not coach, sell, promise an outcome, or talk about other restaurants.

## Part 2. Before the visit (desk review, Form A)

In plain words: before anyone visits, the restaurant sends us its **evidence package**, the recipe for each dish and a label photo or
specification sheet for every ingredient in it. **Form A** is the desk reviewer's checklist for reading that package. The visit only
happens once the package passes.

1. The evidence package arrived at least 10 business days before the visit. If not, the visit moves.
2. Form A's 12 application checks, then its 12 checks for each dish. For every ingredient: brand and product named, a label photo or
   specification sheet. A distributor's letter alone fails G1.
3. Trace what hides in process (G2): stocks, bases, doughs, breads and buns, glazes, sauces, dressings, pan release spray, frying oil,
   garnishes, toppings.
4. Hard stops (fail): honey, beeswax, carmine, shellac, gelatin, isinglass, animal rennet; any label reading "Contains milk" or
   "Contains egg".
5. Needs product proof before it passes: natural flavors, wine, beer, vinegar, vitamin D3, sugar, enzymes, glycerin, emulsifiers,
   fermentation media. Not resolved by the visit: the dish does not pass. The restaurant may send the proof and submit it again.
6. "May contain" is not a fail; it goes in the disclosure.
7. Send Form A to the Verifier. The desk reviewer never does the visit.

## Part 3. The visit (Form B)

Arrive at the agreed time. Photos only, no audio. Phones on silent.

1. **Arrival.** Meet the designated manager. Confirm the dishes, the menu count (M), and that nothing changed since the evidence.
2. **Storage.** Walk-in, reach-ins, dry storage. Pull a 3-invoice sample against the evidence. Every unlabeled container: ask what it is
   and see its source. **Scan** each packaged ingredient of the dish with the Vegans Explore scanner (the Scan tool in the Supermarket
   Guide). It flags a product we know, or what Open Food Facts says about it. A red flag (gelatin, honey, "contains milk", a product not
   in the evidence) is written on Form B. The scanner is a quick check, never proof: the label photo is the proof.
3. **Watch each dish made**, start to finish, by the person who usually makes it, at normal service pace.
4. **Staff.** Question at least two staff who make or serve the dishes. Seven critical questions (Form B); one wrong critical answer fails
   G5 for this visit.
5. **Channels (G4).** Check the printed menu, any boards, the website and every delivery app that lists the dishes.
6. **Photo log.** Each storage area, each dish's station, labels of every traced ingredient, the menu key.
7. **Close.** Tell the manager what you observed, not the outcome. The approver decides.
8. **File** Form B to the approver within 2 business days.

## Part 4. The protected path (G3), what passes and what fails

For A6's review. Every certified dish has a protected cooking path.

| Situation | Ruling |
|---|---|
| Dedicated pan, pot, utensils, cutting board for the dish | Pass |
| A shared pan, cleaned and sanitized, used only for the dish during its cook | Pass |
| A barrier (clean sheet, foil, a dedicated tray) between the dish and a shared surface | Pass |
| Shared fryer oil with any animal product | **Fail** |
| Pasta or vegetable water shared with animal products | **Fail** |
| Grill or flat-top used for meat, not cleaned before the dish | **Fail** |
| Shared oven, dish covered or on its own tray, under a written procedure we approved | Pass |
| Shared oven, no procedure | Fail |
| Tongs or ladles moving between the dish and animal products | Fail |
| Garnish station shared with cheese, butter or meat, no separation | Fail |
| Storage above raw animal products, uncovered | Fail |

When the rule does not cover what you see, write down what you saw and mark it for the approver. Never decide a new case at the counter.

## Part 5. After

- Approved dishes go into the Depot (Depot > Pre-Vegan Certification): the approver moves them to active with the reason; the move needs a
  current term, the menu count and both of you named.
- The public page shows the date each dish was last checked.
- Failed dishes stay in the file. They never show publicly.
- Records stay for the term and 5 years. The system refuses deletes.

## Part 6. Courtesy checks, secret shoppers and reports

- **Courtesy check.** Once in every 12-month term, included in the $250. A short visit: order the dish, look at the station, ask one staff
  member the critical questions. Use the courtesy section of Form B.
- **Secret shoppers.** Between visits, trained members order the dish and ask, "Is this Vegan?" Staff must answer that it is 100%
  plant-based and point to the status page. A wrong answer is reported like a failed spot check.
- **Diner reports.** Anyone can report a problem from the status page or the restaurant's listing, without giving their name. The approver
  decides whether to send a Verifier.

## Part 7. Suspension triggers you report the same day

A changed dish served without telling us first (the restaurant must report every change before it is served; a hidden change ends every
certification at the location and removes the restaurant from Vegans Explore for 2 years, so report exactly what you saw); a protected-path failure on a spot check; a wrong critical answer on a spot check; the mark used on a
dish that is not active; the mark on the door or the whole menu; falsified evidence. You report; the approver suspends.

## Part 8. Training: how a member becomes a Verifier

Open to Vegans Explore members, city by city. Paid work as a contractor, never points.

1. **Apply.** Say which city, what food or kitchen experience you have, and any restaurant you have worked for or advised in the last
   24 months (you never check those).
2. **Modules, at your own pace** (B7b builds them), each ending in a short quiz:
   - M1 The job and the rules (this Part 1, independence, no free food)
   - M2 The eight gates
   - M3 Reading an evidence package (Form A, labels, hidden ingredients)
   - M4 The protected path (Part 4)
   - M5 The visit, step by step (Form B, the scanner, photos)
   - M6 Staff questions and channels (G4, G5)
   - M7 After the visit, courtesy checks, reports and suspensions
3. **Quizzes.** Pass each one to open the next. Up to **3 tries** per quiz; after the third, the team reviews before you try again.
4. **The ring test** below.
5. **One shadow visit** alongside an experienced Verifier or the approver.
6. **Approved** by the team to work solo. The ring test is taken again every year.

## Part 9. The ring test

Every Verifier and desk reviewer works the same ten case files on their own. Each file is a dish with its evidence and visit notes; the
answer key is set by the approver and A6 before the test.

| Case | What is in it | Right answer |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Chickpea curry, every ingredient traced, dedicated pot | Pass |
| 2 | Same curry, vegetable stock with no brand named | Fail G1 |
| 3 | Black bean burger, bun from a supplier, no label photo | Fail G1 |
| 4 | Fries cooked in the shared fryer with chicken | Fail G3 |
| 5 | Stir-fry on a flat-top cleaned before the dish, observed | Pass |
| 6 | Salad with a house dressing that includes honey | Fail G1 |
| 7 | Pasta cooked in the shared pasta water | Fail G3 |
| 8 | Dish passed every gate, one server says "it has a little butter" | Fail G5 |
| 9 | Delivery app lists the dish as "Vegan" with no key | Fail G4 |
| 10 | Sauce with "natural flavors", supplier letter confirms no animal source for that product | Pass |

**Pass:** 9 of 10 correct, and **no case passed that should fail** (a false pass of any case is an automatic retrain). Results are kept
by person and compared across Verifiers each year; a case most people miss means the manual changes.
