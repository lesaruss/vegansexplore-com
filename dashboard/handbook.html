The Academy · Community Manager Track

Community Manager Handbook

The complete training handbook for VEGANS EXPLORE Community Managers, straight from Sean, module by module.

Module 1: What Is a Community Manager

The core truth: without someone taking responsibility for visibility, the work happening in a community effectively disappears, even if it is real and thriving. If there is no platform or presence showing that work, people have no reason to believe there is anything active to support, so engagement dies quietly, not because the community failed, but because nobody could see it.

The Community Manager's job, at its core: make people aware that others are out there working toward this cause in their city and that those people need their support. Be the constant reminder, regardless of what is happening in someone's life, that shows up, an event, a post, a message, and reminds them there is something real they can plug back into.

Practically, day one: get full awareness of every opportunity currently active in your city. Enterprise and sponsor-level opportunities, once those relationships are locked in, arrive as project briefs pushed to your dashboard, giving specific detail on what that particular opportunity requires of you.

Compensation and Path to Paid Role

Two tracks run at once. When a bigger campaign or project comes in, enterprise deals, sponsors, whatever has been locked in, there is always a line item in that budget specifically for the Community Manager, paid directly out of that. Separate from that, the ongoing day-to-day work (everything below) is funded through membership revenue and paid on commission for now, securing your spot as a volunteer/affiliate while your city builds, until membership revenue there grows enough to fund a stipend, and eventually a full salary once it justifies a full-time role.

Module 2: Finding and Onboarding Vegan Businesses

There is (or will be) a directory of every vegan business in your area, The LESARUSS Directory. Your job is to make sure those businesses are in the directory and reach out to them to claim their listing, that outreach is the beginning of the relationship. Templates and resources are provided for that outreach, you are there to be a real human point of contact.

Once a business claims their listing, they get an official email address used through their own dashboard, all communications and CRM happen there. Some of the process is automated (an agent handles initial steps); anything that specifically needs you shows up in your queue as a notification, click it and follow the instructions inside. Three roles in the process: the agent (automation), the business (the customer), and you (the human touchpoint).

VEGANS EXPLORE itself stays a top-of-funnel, nonprofit-facing layer, awareness, coordination, front-end visibility, roughly 80 percent free to use. Once you determine a business genuinely wants a commercial relationship, that business moves to The LESARUSS Directory / LESARUSS AI layer, that is where paid services (marketing, site management, directory placement) actually get pitched and sold, not while still in VE's own front-of-house layer. As opportunities arise there, it becomes your role to make that happen, and commission is earned on whatever closes, same compensation logic as above.

Module 3: Recruiting and Supporting Vegan Explorers

Who to look for: people already doing the work before you find them. Hosting workshops, volunteering at events, posting on social media, showing up consistently. Festivals are the single best place to find them in person, that is why VE needs to be at festivals networking and connecting, not just tabling. The other reliable source is your own events: the people who keep showing up when you host are already self-selecting.

A lot of the time they find you first, not the other way around. The people you are looking for show up right in front of you, at a festival table, in your event crowd, in your comments. The job is recognizing them in the moment and moving fast: get them signed up and into the ecosystem immediately, do not let that moment pass.

Once they are in, the system is supposed to carry the ongoing relationship, not your memory. Automation handles the routine touchpoints; when a real human moment is needed, it surfaces in your queue as something to reach out about. Your job is not to manually track every Explorer, it is to be ready to act on what shows up in front of you.

Churn Is Normal, Consistency Is the Job

People come and go. Do not get locked into any one person sticking around forever, that is not the model. What has to stay constant is you, that consistency is specifically why the role exists. Your job is to make people stick around for as long as they realistically can, not to guarantee they never leave.

Because VE is a multi-city, multi-community system, someone leaving your city is not a pure loss. If an Explorer moves or drops off, they can hop straight into another VE community elsewhere. That cuts both ways in your favor: you also gain people moving in from other communities. Net effect across the system is closer to neutral or positive, not a leak.

Module 4: Running and Attending Events

The website houses every event happening in your city, so there is a standing expectation baked into the role: you are either physically at each of those events, or you have delegated it to a volunteer or Vegan Explorer on your team who is. Either way, someone from VE is in the room.

At the event itself, the job is to make sure everybody there knows about Vegans Explore, ideally converting them into members, plus running some fun on-site activation and connecting people to the digital side of the platform while they are physically present. If you do not already have a relationship with the event owner, building one is part of the job, not optional.

After the event, reporting is expected: capture content (photos, video), enough to put together a short recap or article, either you do this directly or have dedicated people on your team who do. That content becomes part of the regular outgoing content stream, both so people who missed it know these things are happening and can show up to the next one, and so the event owner sees VE showed up and delivered.

Coordinating VE's Own Events

When it is a VE-hosted event rather than someone else's event, you are the coordinator: pulling the team together, sourcing opportunities, and managing whatever logistics the event requires. You are the direct liaison with headquarters, receiving directives from Sean or senior leadership, and when Sean or a senior leader is coming in person, your job is making sure everything is set up, connected, and ready to receive them.

This applies across festivals, outdoor events, and indoor events. Local event storage gets funded and built once your city lands a sponsorship or vendor relationship, at which point it becomes a real budget line item. Ahead of that, a per-event-type SOP will lay out exactly what each event type entails and what each team member on-site is responsible for.

Module 5: Escalation, Authority, and Structure

The main structural touchpoint is a weekly check-in with all Community Managers present at once, not one-on-ones. It functions as a hot lab: each city reports what is working and what is not, questions get asked in front of the group, and HQ gives updates on big projects and opportunities coming down the pipe. Longer term, once a Community Coordinator role exists, hired up from within the CM ranks, that person runs the weekly meeting day to day, and Sean's time is reserved for bigger, less frequent state-of-the-nation conversations with the full team.

Day-to-day, your authority is deliberately prescribed: what is on your plate today is spelled out, it is not an open mandate to decide direction. The weekly meeting is where new ideas are allowed to surface, but on purpose they are not decided or built in a silo by one city. They get raised to the whole group so the team can react, collaborate, and build the idea together, because anything you build is part of the shared VEGANS EXPLORE ecosystem, not a personal side project.

Built for the Long Haul

The direction this role is headed: a real full-time job that pays enough to make it worth building your career here rather than anywhere else, a full-time employee of LESARUSS, earning a real wage, running your city, continuously bringing in revenue, managing the process, being the face, at events, with things booked throughout the week, on Zoom calls. At that level you are effectively managing a portfolio of accounts, anything from a single promotional ad to fully managing a business's marketing, functioning as a kind of fractional CMO for the businesses in your territory. There is only one Community Manager per region, but you build a support system of volunteers and Explorers around yourself, you are not doing it alone.

Every city also has someone working alongside and learning from its Community Manager, so no city ever has to start from zero. That person is training to become a Community Coordinator, and part of their job is being ready to step in if it's ever needed.

Module 6: Performance Standards

Doing well as a Community Manager is responsive and proactive, and it shows up as measurable signals rather than a gut feeling. Reports are due at set times, which roll up into a dashboard visible to HQ across every city. Separately, the platform monitors the health of the vegan businesses connected to your community at scale and produces a score, you'll need to keep that score inside a certain range, that is a direct read on how well you're managing your portfolio.

Beyond the numbers, good performance looks like: doing the big tasks asked of you, showing up to events or making sure there is always coverage when you cannot be there personally, processing your reports on time, and staying actively engaged with the agents assigned to you on the back end, since you use the same platform tooling HQ uses, built to be as easy for you as possible. Community feedback is tracked too, members can flag issues directly, and that feeds into the same picture. On top of all of that, month-over-month growth in your community's size is monitored directly as a hard number.

The short version: your job is to keep your community at the top of the charts across communication, business health, event coverage, community sentiment, and growth. Think of it like being a principal at a school, you get real latitude to shape culture and gather your community, but that autonomy comes with following the rules HQ sets, it is not unconditional.

Module 7: Code of Conduct

Tardiness

Zero tolerance. Showing up even one minute late is unacceptable, and it counts against you, potentially leading to removal after a review.

Review Cadence Is Seasonal, Not Quarterly

Every LESARUSS brand operates in seasons, not calendar quarters, tied to the 111 Day Theory. Community Managers get reviewed at season boundaries: is this still a good fit going forward. Because the date is fixed and known in advance, you'll already know where you stand well before the season ends, if the direction is changing, that conversation and transition planning starts before the cutoff, not as a surprise on the day itself.

Professional Boundaries With Money on the Line

You're expected to build real, authentic relationships with the people in your community, that connection is part of the job. At the same time there has to be a deliberate separation, because you'll be handling money and will sometimes have to deliver news that lets someone down, a deal falling through, a payment issue, something not working out. That has to be able to happen without the personal relationship getting in the way. The standard: build the relationship, but be able to flip the switch and be stern and professional the moment it is a business matter, most concretely, a missed payment.

Module 8: Assessment and Certification

Certification means going through every module and passing the assessments attached to them, with unlimited retries, but a real bar: eighty percent or above to pass. Clearing all of it earns you an actual certification, a stated credential that you've gone through the modules and are certified as a Vegans Explore Community Manager.
