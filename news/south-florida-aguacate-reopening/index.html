Aguacate Garden of Love Returns: South Florida's Beloved Vegan Sanctuary Reopens After 18-Month Hiatus

Owner Daney Cabrera brings back the Kendall restaurant that helped anchor South Florida's plant-based movement, with a full menu, refreshed space, and a grand reopening set for April 25.

Vegans Explore Staff
VE South Florida
April 15, 2026
6 min read
Kendall, FL

It is back. After 18 months of closure, Aguacate Garden of Love quietly reopened its doors in Kendall on April 11, and owner Daney Cabrera made the official announcement on April 15. For anyone who has followed South Florida's plant-based restaurant scene over the past several years, this is not just a restaurant reopening. It is the return of a landmark.

Tucked at 12100 SW 43rd Street in the heart of Kendall, Aguacate built a reputation as one of the region's most welcoming vegan spaces, a place where the food was rooted in care and the community that gathered around it felt like family. When it closed in late 2024, many regulars assumed it was gone for good.

What's on the Menu

The full plant-based menu is back, anchored by the dishes that made Aguacate a fixture in the South Florida vegan community. Cabrera has kept the recipes true to their roots while refreshing the presentation for the new chapter.

The grand reopening is scheduled for April 25, and based on the early response from regulars who have already made their way back, Cabrera can expect a full house. South Florida's vegan community has made it clear: Aguacate was missed.

"This place was never just a restaurant. It was a sanctuary. And sanctuaries come back."
Daney Cabrera, Owner - Aguacate Garden of Love

A Sanctuary in Every Sense

The word "sanctuary" is not incidental to Aguacate's identity. Daney Cabrera has consistently operated the space as a hub for South Florida's broader vegan and animal rights community, hosting events that went well beyond the dining room. Vegans Explore has had a front-row seat to that story.

What This Reopening Means for South Florida

South Florida's vegan restaurant scene has grown considerably over the past several years, but established anchors carry a different kind of weight. Aguacate earned its place not just through the quality of its food but through the consistency of its community investment. When spaces like this close, they leave a gap that newer openings, however good, cannot immediately fill.

The April 25 grand reopening gives the broader South Florida vegan community a formal moment to welcome Aguacate back, and early signs suggest that moment will be well-attended. For Daney Cabrera, who has spent years building a space that functions as more than a restaurant, the return represents a second chapter worth getting right.

Vegans Explore will be on the ground for the grand reopening. Follow Aguacate's directory listing for updates, and watch the VE South Florida community for event coverage.
